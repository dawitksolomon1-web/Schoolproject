How you make money: you sell and run the ads; contractors do the work

You own the brand, the ads, the customer and the booking. Vetted independent detailers do the jobs at a flat rate, and you keep the difference. There's no labor on your payroll and no van to buy. Each job pays for itself before you add another detailer.

The trade-off: an owner-operator keeps about 85% of a $420 detail, while you keep about 47%. So you can't out-margin them on one job. You win on volume and on the back end. An owner-operator can do about 2 cars a day and can't absorb ad-driven volume. You can hire more detailers as fast as leads come in.

The offer: a premium entry detail, ceramic sold on the spot, then a membership

Built on Hormozi's value equation. Raise the dream outcome (a showroom-clean car) and the buyer's confidence (guarantee, insurance, photo report). Cut the waiting (this week) and the effort (we come to you, with our own water and power).

Front end: gets the customer in

The Signature Reset

$349 sedan · $419 SUV · $489 truck/3-row

  • Full interior: vacuum, steam, leather clean and condition, glass
  • Exterior: hand wash, iron and clay decontamination, 6-month sealant
  • Wheels, tires and wheel wells; door jambs
  • Before/after photo report texted to you

Guarantee: if it isn't the cleanest your car has been since you bought it, we come back and redo it free. Still not happy? You don't pay.

Bonuses: headlight restoration or engine bay detail, plus a paint health check.

Scarcity: a limited number of slots per area each week. This is honest, because it's your detailers' real capacity.

Upsell: sold at the car

Ceramic protection

$999 · $1,499 · $2,199 (1, 3 and 5-year tiers)

The paint health check is the opening. The detailer shows the customer the swirls and water spots under a light and offers the coating. Ads never have to sell a $1,500 job, because the car makes the case.

Pay the detailer a commission of $100–150 for each ceramic job they close. Without it, contractors won't pitch.

Paint correction comes later, as a separate premium tier, once you have a detailer who can prove the skill. A bad correction on an expensive car becomes a damage claim.

Back end: recurring revenue

Concierge Membership

$249/month for a maintenance detail every two weeks

  • Keeps the car at Reset standard indefinitely
  • Members get priority booking and 15% off ceramic
  • Pitched right after the Reset: "join today and your next visit is free"

About $100 a month in profit per member, and it compounds. Ten members cover a lot of ad spend. Two hundred is a business you could sell.

Ad channels: Google gets the first bookings; Meta lead forms are where you scale

Captures people already searching

Google Search + Local Services Ads

For: people searching "mobile detailing near me" want to book now. Ads rarely get shut down once approved. Fastest first booking. You can target by zip code.

Against: searches are capped. A metro might have a few thousand a month, and extra budget buys nothing. Clicks cost $5–15. You need reviews to win.

Role: capture every search in your area. Budget about $1–3k a month, set by search volume.

Scales with budget

Meta instant lead forms

For: no ceiling on reach. Video sells detailing. Leads run about $15–40. Nobody has to leave Instagram. It's the only channel where outspending competitors actually works.

Against: lower-intent leads, so you must call within 5 minutes. New accounts get banned. Creative wears out every 2–4 weeks.

Role: where the extra budget goes. Use qualifying questions so tire-kickers screen themselves out.

Test in month 2

Meta ads to your own landing page

For: stronger buyers who pay the deposit themselves, with no calling needed.

Against: fewer conversions, and slow page loads cost bookings.

Role: a split test against lead forms, judged on cost per booked job, never cost per lead.

Reducing the risk of a Meta account ban

  • Use a verified Business Manager with two admins, plus a second ad account warmed up with a small spend
  • Raise budgets 20–30% every few days, not 5× overnight
  • Use a consistent payment method and a real business domain with a privacy policy
  • Keep the creative honest (see Proof). Misleading claims are a common cause of restrictions

The funnel: the 5-minute call and the on-site upsell matter most

Call in 5 min

Dispatch

Job goes to the nearest vetted detailer with a checklist

At the car

Paint check, ceramic pitch, membership pitch

Photo QC

Detailer uploads before/after photos; you approve before the payout

Review + referral

Review request within 2 hours; $50 credit for both friends

Speed to lead. Lead-form leads go cold fast. An automatic text goes out within seconds ("Hi Sam, got your request for the 2023 X5, calling you in 2 minutes"), and a person calls right after. At first that person is you. Later it's a commission-based setter.

The call script, in short: confirm the car and its condition, tell them the price for their size, explain the guarantee, offer two time slots, take the deposit by text-to-pay. If they hesitate, that's what the guarantee is for. Never discount; offer a bonus instead.

Hiring detailers: vet three to five, pay flat rates, approve every job by photo

Where to find them

  • Local mobile detailers on Instagram and Google Maps with fewer than 50 reviews. They have skill but no steady flow of jobs
  • Facebook groups for local detailers; Indeed and Craigslist postings for "mobile detailer, contract, steady work"
  • Pay each one for a paid trial on a car you choose before they get customer jobs

Protect the brand

  • A written contractor agreement: quality standard, no taking your customers for themselves, they cover damage up to a limit
  • Require their insurance certificate; carry your own general liability plus coverage for customers' cars while in your care
  • They supply a self-contained rig (water tank and generator), their own products and a branded shirt
  • Photo checklist on every job, with payout after you approve it
  • Contractor vs. employee rules vary by state, so check before scaling

Proof without past jobs: use the detailers' real work, and use AI for everything else

Use this

  • Your detailers' own portfolios, with written permission, labelled as "work by our detailers." It's real, and it's the same people who will do the customer's car
  • Paid trial jobs on cars you pick. These produce your first real before/afters
  • AI (Higgsfield and similar) for hooks, brand and lifestyle scenes, offer cards, motion graphics and b-roll. Use it to test 50 angles at once
  • A founding-client bonus for the first 25 customers: a free add-on in exchange for a review and photo permission

Don't use this

  • AI-generated before/afters presented as your results
  • AI avatars posing as customers, or made-up reviews
  • Other shops' photos used without permission

These are false advertising under FTC rules, can get a Meta ad account restricted, and fall apart when a $1,500 customer compares the ad with the real result. Real proof builds up with every job, so after about 20 jobs this stops being an issue.
